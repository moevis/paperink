# Fraction Arithmetic

## Reciprocals

**Two numbers whose product is $1$ are reciprocals of each other.** To take a reciprocal, swap the numerator and the denominator.

$$\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{b}{a} = 1$$

- The reciprocal of $\frac{2}{3}$ is $\frac{3}{2}$
- The reciprocal of an integer $n$ is $\frac{1}{n}$
- The reciprocal of $1$ is $1$
- $0$ has no reciprocal (multiplying it by anything still gives $0$, never $1$)

## Multiplication

$$\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \times c}{b \times d}$$

Multiply numerators together and denominators together; no common denominator needed.

$$\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{4}{5} = \frac{8}{15}$$

Reduce first when you can (cross-cancel):

$$\frac{3}{8} \times \frac{4}{9} = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{6}$$

## Division

Dividing by a number means multiplying by its reciprocal.

$$\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c}$$

$$\frac{5}{6} \div \frac{10}{3} = \frac{5}{6} \times \frac{3}{10} = \frac{1}{4}$$

## Addition

With the same denominator, keep the denominator and add the numerators:

$$\frac{a}{c} + \frac{b}{c} = \frac{a + b}{c}$$

$$\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{3}$$

With different denominators, rewrite both over a common denominator first:

$$\frac{a}{b} + \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \times d}{b \times d} + \frac{b \times c}{b \times d}$$

$$\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{3}{6} + \frac{2}{6} = \frac{5}{6}$$

## Subtraction

Just like addition, except the numerators are subtracted. Same rule: get a common denominator first.

$$\frac{7}{8} - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{21}{24} - \frac{4}{24} = \frac{17}{24}$$

## Reduction

Dividing numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number leaves the value unchanged.

$$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{a \div k}{b \div k}$$

$$\frac{12}{18} = \frac{2}{3}$$

The answer is only finished once it is in **lowest terms** (numerator and denominator coprime).

## Mixed numbers

Convert to an improper fraction before doing arithmetic.

$$2\frac{1}{3} = \frac{2 \times 3 + 1}{3} = \frac{7}{3}$$

## Where marks get lost

**1. You cannot add numerators across different denominators**

$\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{5}{6}$, not $\frac{2}{5}$.

**2. With equal denominators the denominator does not get added**

$\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{3}$, not $\frac{2}{6}$.

**3. A reciprocal is not the opposite**

The opposite of $\frac{2}{3}$ is $-\frac{2}{3}$; its reciprocal is $\frac{3}{2}$.

Swapping only turns the fraction around — **the sign stays put**: the reciprocal of $-\frac{2}{3}$ is $-\frac{3}{2}$.

**4. The trouble with $0$**

$\frac{0}{5} = 0$, but $0$ has no reciprocal; and $\frac{5}{0}$ is meaningless to begin with.

**5. Bracket the top and bottom of the fraction separately**

$\frac{1+2}{3+4} = \frac{3}{7}$, not $1 + \frac{2}{3} + 4$.

## Worked examples

**Example 1** Evaluate $\frac{1}{4} + \frac{2}{3} \times \frac{3}{8}$

Multiplication first:

$$\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{3}{8} = \frac{1}{4}$$

Then the addition:

$$\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$$

**Example 2** Two thirds of a number is $12$; find the number

$$12 \div \frac{2}{3} = 12 \times \frac{3}{2} = 18$$

**Example 3** Compare $\frac{5}{6}$ with $\frac{7}{8}$

Rewrite both over $24$:

$$\frac{5}{6} = \frac{20}{24}
\qquad
\frac{7}{8} = \frac{21}{24}$$

Since $21 > 20$, we get $\frac{7}{8} > \frac{5}{6}$.

**Example 4** Evaluate $\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{9}{16}$

$$\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{9}{16} = \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{16}{9} = \frac{4}{3}$$

---

Multiply and divide directly; add and subtract over a common denominator; reduce at the end.
